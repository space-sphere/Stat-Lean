    Data-processing inequality for KL divergence under a measurable map #

    For a measurable map $f : \alpha \to \beta$ and probability measures $\mu, \nu$ on $\alpha$, the Kullback–Leibler divergence contracts under the push-forward (deterministic channel): $$ D\!\left(\mu \circ f^{-1} \,\big\|\, \nu \circ f^{-1}\right) \;\le\; D(\mu \,\|\, \nu). $$ This is the elementary deterministic-channel case of the data-processing inequality for an $f$-divergence: processing the data through any measurable map can only reduce the KL divergence between the two laws. It is the key contraction step used in Wainwright's proof of Pinsker's inequality (Exercise 15.6), where one reduces the general KL divergence to the two-cell Bernoulli case via the indicator push-forward of the level set $\{q \le p\}$.

    Reference. M. J. Wainwright, High-Dimensional Statistics: A Non-Asymptotic Viewpoint, Cambridge University Press, 2019. Chapter 15 (Minimax lower bounds), §15.1.3 (Some divergence measures), used in Exercise 15.6 (Pinsker's inequality).

    Proof formalization notes. Mathlib provides the kernel chain rule klDiv_compProd_left but not the push-forward data-processing inequality, so we add klDiv_map_le here as a reusable ForMathlib brick (candidate upstream); it is consumed by PinskerInequality.lean. The proof realizes $\mu \circ f^{-1}$ as a marginal of $\mu \otimes (\text{deterministic } f)$ and runs through the integral representation of the divergence: writing $D(\mu\|\nu) = \int \mathrm{klFun}(d\mu/d\nu)\, d\nu$, the push-forward density $d(\mu \circ f^{-1})/d(\nu \circ f^{-1}) \circ f$ equals the conditional expectation of $d\mu/d\nu$ given the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $f$ (toReal_rnDeriv_map), so conditional Jensen for the convex function $\mathrm{klFun}$ on $[0,\infty)$ (convexOn_klFun.map_condExp_le) yields the inequality. The degenerate cases ($D(\mu\|\nu) = \infty$, or $\mu \not\ll \nu$) are handled separately by le_top.

    Bibliographic comments. The data-processing inequality for KL divergence (and more generally for any $f$-divergence) is classical folklore with no single seminal source. The general $f$-divergence statement and its convexity proof are due to I. Csiszár, Information-type measures of difference of probability distributions and indirect observations, Studia Scientiarum Mathematicarum Hungarica 2 (1967), 299–318, and independently S. M. Ali and S. D. Silvey, A general class of coefficients of divergence of one distribution from another, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B, 28 (1966), 131–142. The KL-specific case is the standard data-processing inequality of information theory; see T. M. Cover and J. A. Thomas, Elements of Information Theory, 2nd ed., Wiley, 2006, Theorem 2.8.1 (data-processing inequality for mutual information / relative entropy).

    Data-processing inequality for KL divergence under a measurable map. For a measurable f : α → β and probability measures μ, ν, the KL divergence between the push-forwards is at most the KL divergence between the originals: klDiv (μ.map f) (ν.map f) ≤ klDiv μ ν. This is the standard contraction of an f-divergence under a (deterministic) channel; proved from the kernel chain rule klDiv_compProd_left by realizing μ.map f as a marginal of μ ⊗ₘ deterministic f, since Mathlib lacks a general data-processing inequality.

    Reference. Wainwright, High-Dimensional Statistics: A Non-Asymptotic Viewpoint, Cambridge University Press, 2019. Chapter 15 (Minimax lower bounds), §15.1.3 (Some divergence measures).
