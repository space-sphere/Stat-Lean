    Markov's inequality and the Chernoff bound #

    Two foundational tail bounds. Both are reference results in Lu's §4.2 setup for sub-Gaussian theory but are themselves general (no sub-Gaussian hypothesis).

    Markov's inequality. For a nonnegative integrable random variable $X$ and a threshold $t > 0$, $$ \mathbb{P}(X \ge t) \;\le\; \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{t}. $$ The book states this with the strict event $\{X > t\}$; the inequality $\le$ is preserved by enlarging the event to $\{X \ge t\}$, so the form proved here ($t \le X$) majorises and is strictly stronger than the book's version.

    Chernoff bound. Let $\psi(\lambda) = \log \mathbb{E}\big[e^{\lambda (X - \mathbb{E}[X])}\big]$ be the centered cumulant generating function (log-MGF) and let $\psi^*(t) = \sup_{\lambda \ge 0}\big(\lambda t - \psi(\lambda)\big)$ be its Fenchel–Legendre dual. Then for every $t \ge 0$, $$ \mathbb{P}\big(X - \mathbb{E}[X] \ge t\big) \;\le\; e^{-\psi^*(t)}. $$ The Lean statement makes no sign assumption on $t$, so it is strictly stronger than the book's $t \ge 0$ version (for $t < 0$ the bound may be vacuous, but it remains valid).

    The Chernoff statement carries two side conditions that are forced by the Lean formalisation rather than the book text (see the -- LEAN-ONLY tags on the hypotheses): a uniform MGF-finiteness hypothesis on $[0, \infty)$ (so the per-$\lambda$ exponential Markov step applies at every $\lambda$), and a bounded-above hypothesis on $\{\lambda t - \psi(\lambda) : \lambda \ge 0\}$ (so the real-valued supremum defining $\psi^*$ agrees with the textbook sup; otherwise it falls back on the junk value $0$, making $e^{-\psi^*} = 1$ and the bound vacuous).

    Reference. Junwei Lu, Big Data Analysis, Springer Nature Switzerland, 2025 (ISBN 978-3-032-03160-0), Chapter 4 (Concentration Inequalities), §4.2 (Sub-Gaussian Random Variables): Markov's inequality and Theorem 4.4 (Chernoff Bound).

    Proof formalization notes.

    • markov — a thin wrapper around Mathlib's MeasureTheory.mul_meas_ge_le_integral_of_nonneg, proving the $t \le X$ form (which majorises the book's strict $X > t$ form).

    • chernoff — for each $\lambda \ge 0$, Mathlib's ProbabilityTheory.measure_ge_le_exp_cgf (Markov applied to the exponential moment) gives $\mathbb{P}(X - \mathbb{E}[X] \ge t) \le e^{-\lambda t + \psi(\lambda)} = e^{-(\lambda t - \psi(\lambda))}$. Taking the infimum over $\lambda \ge 0$: since $x \mapsto e^{-x}$ is antitone and continuous, $\inf_{\lambda \ge 0} e^{-(\lambda t - \psi(\lambda))} = e^{-\psi^*(t)}$. This last step is delegated to Antitone.map_csSup_of_continuousAt on Real.exp ∘ Neg.neg. The two -- LEAN-ONLY side conditions described above guard the formalisation; there is no book-vs-Lean constant deviation.

    Bibliographic comments. Markov's inequality is classical folklore with no single seminal paper; it is named after A. A. Markov and generalises the bound of his teacher P. L. Chebyshev (Chebyshev, "Des valeurs moyennes", Journal de mathématiques pures et appliquées, 1867). The Chernoff bound — optimising the exponential Markov inequality over the tilt parameter $\lambda$ — originates with H. Chernoff, "A measure of asymptotic efficiency for tests of a hypothesis based on the sum of observations", Annals of Mathematical Statistics 23 (1952), no. 4, 493–507; the exponential-rate / large-deviation argument appears there in the analysis of error probabilities (Chernoff credits the key inequality to Herman Rubin). The Legendre-dual rate function $\psi^*$ is the same object that governs Cramér's large-deviation theorem.

        theorem StatLean.ConcentrationInequalities.chernoff {Ω : Type u_1} {mΩ : MeasurableSpace Ω} {X : Ω → ℝ} {μ : MeasureTheory.Measure Ω} [MeasureTheory.IsProbabilityMeasure μ] {t : ℝ} (hInt : ∀ (lam : ℝ), 0 ≤ lam → MeasureTheory.Integrable (fun (ω : Ω) => Real.exp (lam * (X ω - ∫ (x : Ω), X x ∂μ))) μ) (hBdd : BddAbove (Set.range fun (lam : { lam : ℝ // 0 ≤ lam }) => ↑lam * t - psi X μ ↑lam)) :
        μ.real {ω : Ω | t ≤ X ω - ∫ (x : Ω), X x ∂μ} ≤ Real.exp (-psiStar X μ t)

        Chernoff bound (Lu-BDA §4.2, Theorem 4.4 (Chernoff Bound)): with the centered log-MGF ψ(λ) = log E[exp(λ (X − E[X]))] and its Legendre dual ψ*(t) = ⨆_{λ ≥ 0} (λ t − ψ(λ)), P(X − E[X] ≥ t) ≤ exp(−ψ*(t)).

        The book states this for t ≥ 0; the Lean form is strictly stronger as it makes no sign assumption on t (for t < 0 the bound may be vacuous because ψ*(t) can be small or non-positive, but it is still valid).

        Proof sketch: for each λ ≥ 0, Mathlib's measure_ge_le_exp_cgf (Markov on the exponential moment) gives P(X − E X ≥ t) ≤ exp(−λ t + ψ(λ)) = exp(−(λ t − ψ(λ))). Take the infimum over λ ≥ 0; since x ↦ exp(−x) is antitone and continuous, inf_{λ ≥ 0} exp(−(λ t − ψ(λ))) = exp(−ψ*(t)).
