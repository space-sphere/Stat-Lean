    The Lipschitz law of large numbers — Theorem 8.2.3, assembly #

    For i.i.d. $X_1, \dots, X_n$ with values in $[0,1]$ and common law $P$, the empirical process is uniformly small over the genuine full $L$-Lipschitz class: $$ \mathbb{E} \sup_{\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} \le L} \Bigl| \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(X_i) - \mathbb{E}_P f \Bigr| \;\le\; \frac{C L}{\sqrt{n}}, \qquad C = 960. $$ No countability hypothesis appears: the supremum inside the expectation is over the uncountable subtype $\{f : [0,1] \to \mathbb{R} \,\|\, \|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} \le L\}$.

    Reference. Roman Vershynin, High-Dimensional Probability, 2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, §8.2, Theorem 8.2.3 (book constant $C$ unnamed) and Eq. (8.25).

    Proof formalization notes.
